Centerville sits in the east-central part of Hamden and takes its name from the crossing of Whitney Avenue and Dixwell Avenue, the town's two main thoroughfares. Hamden's neighborhoods have no official boundaries, but one common map runs Centerville from James and Forest streets south to Skiff Street, between Dixwell and Evergreen avenues and the Mill River, which runs mostly along the North Haven town line. It uses ZIP code 06518.

Commercial and civic buildings line the main avenues, and the rest is residential, with single-family houses, condominiums and apartments. That mix is the reason this page is built around one question. Who owns the pipe that is leaking?

Whose pipe is it? Houses, condos and rentals

The right first call depends on where the problem is and who owns that part of the building. The table below gives the usual pattern, but your lease, condo declaration or deed decides it.

Your situationUsually handled byWhat to do first
Single-family houseYou, the ownerFind your main shutoff and call a plumber.

What is documented about Centerville

The area was first settled in the early 1700s. A mill race built around 1820 near the Mill River crossing brought industry, and in 1835 Centerville became the seat of town government. Hamden Memorial Town Hall, listed on the National Register of Historic Places, stands on Dixwell Avenue. The Miller Memorial Library, Town Center Park and Hamden Middle School are also here.

Because Town Hall and the Government Center are close, it is easy to ask the Permits and Licensing Center a question in person. Plumbing work in Connecticut follows the 2022 State Building Code, which is based on the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC).

Before you call a plumber in a rental or condo

Take a photo of the problem and note where the water is coming from. Find the shutoff valve for your unit if you can. Then contact your landlord or property manager, because many leases and condo documents require that step before any repair.

Utilities and landmarks in Centerville

Water in Hamden is supplied by the South Central Connecticut Regional Water Authority, which draws most of its supply from reservoirs.
