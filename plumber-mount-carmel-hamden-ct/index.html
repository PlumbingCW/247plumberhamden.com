Mount Carmel is a neighborhood in the northeastern part of Hamden. Hamden has no official neighborhood boundaries, but one common map places it between the Cheshire and Wallingford town lines on the north and James and Forest streets on the south. The North Haven line marks the east and the Farmington Canal Heritage Trail marks the west. It uses ZIP code 06518.

It is primarily residential, mostly single-family homes, with commercial development along Whitney Avenue. Its name comes from the resemblance of the nearby hills to the biblical Mount Carmel, and the Sleeping Giant hills now form a state park with about 30 miles of trails.

Start by finding out what your house connects to

In a large, spread-out neighborhood the first plumbing question is often not about the pipe. It is about where the pipe goes. Some northern Hamden properties may be on a private septic system instead of the municipal sewer, and reporting in 2018 described parts of Whitney Avenue north of the center as largely unsewered. We have not verified that for individual streets, so confirm yours.

Water also varies. The Regional Water Authority supplies most of the town from reservoirs and also uses some wells. Your water bill and the town's records show which system serves you.

Quinnipiac University and landlords nearby

Quinnipiac University's main campus is in Mount Carmel, and it moved here from Whitneyville in 1966. If you manage a rental property near campus, tell us the number of units and who gives us access. Clear information up front keeps a repair from becoming several visits. See commercial plumbing for multi-unit buildings and offices.

Plumbing work in Connecticut follows the 2022 State Building Code, which is based on the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC). Septic systems are regulated separately, so ask the town health authority before any septic work.

What to do first if a pipe freezes or bursts

Pipes on exterior walls are where freezing usually starts, so these steps matter most on cold nights.

  1. Close the main water shutoff as soon as you can reach it.
  2. Open a faucet below the damage so the pipe can drain.
  3. Keep people and pets away from wet electrical equipment.
  4. Call a plumber and take photos of the damage for your records.
