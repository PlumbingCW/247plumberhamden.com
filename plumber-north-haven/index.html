What North Haven homeowners call about first

Drain and sewer backups lead the list in a town built up quickly after the war. A kitchen sink that will not clear is often a grease problem near the sink, while a basement floor drain that gurgles points deeper, toward the lateral or the main. Our emergency drain cleaning team starts with an auger and moves to a camera when the clog keeps returning.

Water heater failures come second, usually a leaking tank found on the basement floor. If you see water at the base of the tank, shut off the cold supply and call. Our water heater repair page explains when a repair makes sense and when replacement saves money.

North Haven at a glance

Where it sits

North Haven borders Hamden, New Haven, East Haven, Branford and Wallingford, with the Quinnipiac River running through its sand plains.

Population

The 2020 Census counted 24,253 residents in about 21 square miles.

Sewers

The Public Works Department runs the town's wastewater collection and treatment under the Water Pollution Control Authority.

Dispatch

Emergency dispatch is available around the clock from Hamden, and we ask for your cross streets first so we can route the closest plumber.

A postwar town and what that means for the pipes

North Haven's population roughly quadrupled between 1945 and 1970, spurred by employers such as Pratt & Whitney and Marlin Firearms. Many neighborhoods, including Montowese and Green Acres, grew in that stretch, so a large share of the housing is now more than half a century old.

Homes of that age often still have original drain lines and old water heaters, so the practical takeaway is simple. If your home dates from the postwar years and your drains slow down regularly, a sewer camera inspection is cheaper than repeated clearing.

Sand, river and basement water

The Quinnipiac River valley gives North Haven its sandy soils, and groundwater can sit closer to basement floors than it does on Hamden's ridges. After heavy rain, a sump pump that fails quickly becomes a flooded basement.

Test yours before storm season by pouring a bucket of water into the pit. Our sump pump repair and backup installation team handles both failed pumps and battery backups, and we can talk through basement waterproofing options if water keeps returning.

North Haven sewers and who to call

North Haven Public Works administers the town's sewer program and lists its office at the Town Hall Annex, 5 Linsley Street, with a phone line at (203) 239-5321, extension 8400, on weekdays. That office handles the public sewer, while the private lateral from your house to the main is the property owner's responsibility in most places.

If a backup affects several neighbors at once, tell Public Works and call us for the pipe on your property. Permit rules vary, so ask the town before any excavation. Plumbing work here follows the 2022 Connecticut State Building Code, which is based on the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC).

ZIP 06473 and the places we serve

North Haven is served as one ZIP, 06473, from the Green and the Route 5 corridor through Clintonville to Ridge Road. Quinnipiac University's graduate campus on Bassett Road and the businesses along Route 5 also fall inside that area, and our commercial plumbing crews work with them after hours when needed.

East Haven sits just to the south, and we also take calls there. For towns to the north, see Wallingford and for the city to the south, see New Haven.
