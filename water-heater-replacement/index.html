Water heater replacement in Hamden, CT is the right answer when a tank has failed, when it leaks from the shell, or when the repair would cost more than the unit is worth. If you woke up to a wet floor or a cold shower, we run emergency water heater replacement on a 24/7 dispatch line, and we aim for a same-day swap when the schedule and the right unit are available.

Replacement means removing the old heater, setting a new one, reconnecting water, gas or electric, venting, drain and relief lines, and testing the finished installation. We keep the job to the new unit and its connections, so you are not paying for extras you did not ask for.

When a failed tank is an emergency

A tank that is pouring water, or one that has gone cold with no sign of a quick fix, is a same-day problem. We take these calls at night and on weekends, and we can usually talk you through the shutoffs over the phone.

Do these first.

  1. Close the cold water inlet valve at the top of the tank.
  2. Turn off power at the breaker for an electric heater, or turn the gas control to off for a gas unit.
  3. Open a hot faucet upstairs to let the tank drain slowly if the leak is serious, and use a hose to drain it outdoors if you can.
  4. If there is a gas smell, leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 first.

Sizing a replacement properly

A unit that is too small leaves you with lukewarm showers when two people are running water. One that is too big wastes energy. We size by household, the number of bathrooms, and your peak usage, not by whatever happens to be on the truck.

Callers often ask whether to size up when the old heater was undersized. If you ran out of hot water regularly, tell us, and we will account for it.

What a clean replacement involves

The old unit is drained, disconnected and removed. We replace worn supply connectors, install a new relief valve and discharge line, and add an expansion tank where the system needs one. Gas units get a venting check and a leak test at the connections.

Plumbing work in Connecticut must meet the 2022 Connecticut State Building Code, which is based on the 2021 International Plumbing Code. Permit rules vary, so ask the Hamden building department whether your swap needs a permit, and we will help you with the details we know.

Hamden homes and the practical limits

Many Hamden homes have the water heater in a basement or utility closet, and a few are in tight spaces behind finished walls. Access affects the time a swap takes, the type of unit that fits, and how we get the old tank out. Tell us about stairs, ceiling height and doorway width when you call.

If your home was built decades ago, older gas and venting arrangements sometimes need updating to meet current code. We check that rather than copy the old setup.

Planned replacement beats emergency replacement

If your tank is old but still running, replace it on your terms. You choose the type, the day and the budget, and you avoid flooding a finished basement. Our water heater installation page covers planned installs and upgrades.

If you are not sure of the unit's age, the serial number on the data plate usually encodes it. We can read it for you on the phone.

What to do when the tank fails

Act in this order, whatever the cause.

  1. Turn off the gas control or the breaker for the heater.
  2. Close the cold water supply valve above the tank.
  3. Open a hot tap to relieve pressure and stop water pooling.
  4. Call us. If you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 first.

Code requirements for a replacement

A replacement is regulated work. Connecticut's 2022 State Building Code, based on the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), covers relief valves, discharge piping and supply connections, and gas and venting carry their own rules. Permit rules vary, so ask the Hamden Permits and Licensing Center whether a swap needs a permit.
