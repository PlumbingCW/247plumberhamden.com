A tankless water heater in Hamden, CT heats water on demand instead of storing it, so you never run out of a tank but you depend on the unit's flow and heat settings. We install tankless models, repair them when they fault, and run the descaling flush that keeps the heat exchanger working for years.

Callers usually reach us for one of three reasons. They want to install one, they have an error code or a sink that will not get hot, or they have been told the unit needs servicing. We service them.

Flush and descale: what it is and why

Minerals in water leave scale on the heat exchanger of a tankless unit. Scale insulates the metal, so the unit works harder and can overheat, throw error codes or reduce flow. A descaling flush circulates a mild acid solution or approved cleaner through the unit to dissolve that scale, and then rinses it out.

Manufacturers commonly recommend flushing at regular intervals, and the right interval depends on your water. We follow the manufacturer's guidance for your model and adjust for what we find when we open the unit.

The kitchen sink problem

A common call is a tankless unit that heats the bathroom well and fails at the kitchen sink. Tankless units have a minimum flow rate below which they will not fire, and a small kitchen faucet aerator can fall under it. Long pipe runs and a crossover between hot and cold lines can also steal heat.

Fixes range from adjusting the aerator to correcting a crossover or adding a recirculation line. We find the cause before suggesting a part.

Venting and gas supply

Tankless gas units draw a lot of fuel and push out hot exhaust through a sealed vent. Both the gas line size and the vent run have to meet the manufacturer's specifications and local code. A unit that was installed on an undersized gas line is a classic reason for hot water that fades under load.

If you are replacing a tank with a tankless unit, we measure the gas line and plan the venting before we recommend it. Our installation page describes the broader planning process.

Water in the Hamden area and your tankless unit

The Regional Water Authority supplies water to much of Hamden, and it publishes an annual report on what is in it. We do not assume a hardness number for your address. Test your tap, or read the authority's report for your service area, and share the result so we can set a flush schedule that fits.

Homes served by a private well are different. If you have a well, test it, because water with high mineral content scales a tankless unit much faster.

Repair or replace

A unit that is a few years old and throwing an error code is almost always worth repairing. A heat exchanger damaged by years of scale, or one that has leaked, may not be. We will tell you where yours stands, and our water heater repair page covers the tank-based path.

Manufacturer warranties often depend on documented maintenance, so keep your service records.

Venting, gas and the code

Tankless units depend on correct venting, a condensate drain and an adequate gas supply, and each is regulated. Connecticut's 2022 State Building Code is based on the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), and the manufacturer's installation instructions are part of the standard a unit must meet. Permit rules vary, so ask the Hamden Permits and Licensing Center.
