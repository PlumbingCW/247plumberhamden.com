Garbage disposal repair in Hamden, CT usually answers one of a few symptoms: the unit hums but does not turn, it will not run at all, it leaks from the bottom or the sides, or the sink drains slowly even when the disposal runs. Most of these have a clear cause and a quick fix.

A disposal that leaks under the sink or backs a sink up into the dishwasher cannot wait for a convenient week. We answer 24/7, dispatch day and night, and try for the earliest same-day visit.

Disposal leaking or sink backing up

Standing water in a cabinet ruins the cabinet floor quickly, and a backed-up sink is unusable. We take after-hours calls.

Do this first.

  1. Switch the disposal off at the wall switch and unplug it or turn off its breaker if you can do so safely.
  2. Close the two stop valves under the sink if a supply line is involved, or the main if they will not turn.
  3. Place a bucket under any drip and clear the cabinet.
  4. Do not run the dishwasher while the sink is backing up.

Humming but not turning

A hum with no rotation means the motor has power but the flywheel is jammed. A bone, a fruit pit or a piece of utensil is the usual cause. With the power off, a hex wrench in the socket at the bottom of the unit can rock the flywheel free, and the reset button on the bottom restores the motor.

Never put your hand in the disposal, even when it is off. Use tongs or pliers to pull out what you can see.

When the clog is not the disposal

A disposal that runs well while the sink drains slowly points to a clog in the kitchen drain beyond the unit. Grease and food residue harden in the pipe, and the disposal only grinds what it receives.

We clear those lines with a drain machine, which is covered under drain cleaning. If the clog returns often, the line may need a camera look.

Repair or replace the unit

A disposal that is seized, leaking from the housing or more than ten years old is usually better replaced than repaired. A new unit is often a direct swap for the same mount, and we can match horsepower and noise to your needs.

If you do not have a disposal now and want one, we install them along with the electrical connection, and we confirm the sink and drain setup will accept it.

What to avoid putting in a disposal

Grease, coffee grounds in quantity, pasta, rice and fibrous scraps such as celery and corn husks can build up and clog the drain. Bones and fruit pits can jam the unit. Run cold water while it works and for a few seconds afterward to carry waste through the line.

If your home is on a septic system, ask before adding a disposal, because the extra solids load the tank.

Hamden kitchens and drains

Older Hamden homes may have cast iron or galvanized kitchen drains that narrow over time, which makes a disposal seem weak when the pipe is the real problem. We tell you which it is before suggesting a replacement.

Much of Hamden is served by the Greater New Haven Water Pollution Control Authority, while 2018 local reporting described northern Hamden along Whitney Avenue as largely on septic, so it is worth knowing which you have.

Code and electrical limits

A disposal connects to the drain, the dishwasher and an electrical supply. Connecticut's 2022 State Building Code is based on the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC) for the plumbing side, and electrical work has its own rules and licensing. We handle the drain connections and recommend a licensed electrician for new wiring.
