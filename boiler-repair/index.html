Boiler repair in Hamden, CT matters most from late autumn into winter, when a boiler that will not fire is a house with no heat. We diagnose hot-water and steam boilers, repair leaks, pumps, valves and controls, and check the safety items that protect your family and your home.

We keep this page honest about scope. If the problem lies in an oil burner, a chimney liner or a specialized control that is better handled by a heating contractor, we tell you directly instead of guessing.

Start with the simple checks

Before you call, a few quick checks can save time. Make sure the thermostat is calling for heat and has fresh batteries if it uses them. Look for a tripped breaker or switch at the boiler and check the fuel supply. Then see whether the boiler's pressure gauge reads in the normal range printed on or near it.

Do not bleed radiators or adjust the boiler beyond what the manufacturer's manual describes. If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 first.

Hot water versus steam

Hot-water boilers push heated water through baseboard or radiators and return it to the boiler. Steam boilers boil water and send steam to radiators, which makes them sensitive to water level, air vents and pipe pitch. Each fails in its own way.

Older homes in Hamden may have either system, and some have had the boiler replaced while the radiators and piping stayed. We identify what you have before we start.

Safety checks we take seriously

A boiler burns fuel, so combustion and venting always matter. A cracked flue, a blocked chimney or a worn burner can send carbon monoxide into the house. We check the venting where we can see it, and we strongly recommend working carbon monoxide alarms on every level of the home.

Our blog on exhaust leaks covers the warning signs in more detail.

Annual service and what it prevents

A boiler that is cleaned and tested before the heating season is less likely to fail in the middle of it. Service typically includes inspecting the burner or heat exchanger, testing safeties, checking the expansion tank and verifying water pressure.

If your boiler has been through several repairs in a few seasons, ask us for an honest comparison of repair costs against replacement.

Where we draw the line

Plumbers and heating technicians overlap, but they are not identical. We handle boiler plumbing, water-side repairs, circulators, valves and leaks. For oil burner work, chimney lining or complex controls, we may refer you to a specialist so the repair is done by the person best equipped for it.

A water leak from a boiler also affects your plumbing, and our leak detection page explains how we trace water that is not obvious.

Who is licensed for what

Boiler work crosses trades. The plumbing side follows Connecticut's 2022 State Building Code, which is based on the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), while burner and gas work carry separate licensing. You can check any contractor in the state's eLicense portal run by the Department of Consumer Protection, and we tell you when a job needs another trade.
