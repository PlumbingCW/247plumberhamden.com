Plumbing installation in Hamden, CT covers the work that adds or moves plumbing: a new bathroom, a relocated kitchen sink, a laundry hookup, an outdoor spigot, a basement bath or a new fixture set. These are planned projects, so we focus on getting the layout right before any pipe is cut.

If your project began because something failed, such as a leaking water heater or a broken fixture, see our pages on water heater replacement or faucet repair first. This page is for projects that you schedule.

What we install

Our installation work includes sinks, faucets, toilets, showers and tubs, garbage disposals, dishwashers and washing machine hookups, hose bibs and shutoff valves. We also run new supply and drain lines for additions, finished basements and converted spaces.

We do not provide the fixtures unless you ask, and we are happy to install ones you have chosen. If you want advice, tell us your budget and your priorities, and we will point you toward models that are easy to service.

Basement bathrooms and the drain question

In a basement, the existing sewer line may be higher than the new fixtures, so gravity drainage will not work. A sewage ejector pit or an upflush toilet system lifts waste to the main drain. Both add equipment, a vent and a power supply, and they need access for service.

Basement finishing is a major reason people call us before the framing is closed. Plumbing rough-in done after the drywall goes up is more expensive and more disruptive, so we recommend a call at the design stage.

Rough-in, inspection and finish

New plumbing happens in stages. Rough-in puts supply and drain lines in the framing, and it is typically inspected and pressure tested before walls close. Finish work sets the fixtures after drywall and flooring are done.

Connecticut plumbing work falls under the 2022 State Building Code, which is based on the 2021 International Plumbing Code. In Hamden, building permits go through the town's permit portal, and we recommend confirming with Town Hall at 203-287-7000 which permits your project needs.

Hamden homes, additions and rentals

Hamden has a mix of older village homes and later development, plus rental properties. Landlords and property managers often call us for repeat fixture installs, and we can schedule around tenants.

If you own a rental, tell us how many units are involved and who handles access. A clear plan keeps the work moving and keeps tenants informed.

Why planned work still benefits from a plumber early

A plumbing layout set by the architect or contractor is not always the layout that works in the walls. Pipe size, slope, venting and access are real constraints, and a short consultation can prevent a change order later.

For larger work or whole-house updates, our repiping page covers replacing supply lines throughout the house.

Code and permits for new plumbing

New and altered plumbing is where the code matters most. Connecticut's 2022 State Building Code is based on the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), which covers fixture requirements, drain slope, venting and testing. Ask the Hamden Permits and Licensing Center which permits your project needs, and verify any contractor in the state's eLicense portal.
