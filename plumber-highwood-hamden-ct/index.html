Highwood is a neighborhood in the south-central part of Hamden, with its commercial development along Dixwell Avenue, most of which is part of Route 10. Hamden's neighborhoods have no official boundaries, but one map places Highwood between Helen Street and Putnam Avenue on the north and the New Haven city line on the south, with Newhall Street on the east and Fitch Street and Fairview Avenue on the west.

The western part uses ZIP code 06514 and the eastern part uses 06517. Highwood should not be confused with the Newhallville neighborhood in New Haven.

A mixed housing stock

Highwood is primarily residential. Housing includes small apartment buildings alongside single-family, two-family and three-family homes. German immigrants settled the area extensively in the 1860s, and Irish, Italian and Eastern European families followed later in the century. A post office opened in 1888 and gave the neighborhood its name.

In buildings with more than one household, plumbing questions are about shared systems as much as pipes. The list below covers what we ask on the phone.

Questions we ask about a multi-unit building

Clear answers up front often mean one visit instead of two.

  • How many units are in the building and which one has the problem?
  • Do the units share a water heater, a boiler or a main drain line?
  • Where is the shutoff for the affected unit, and where is the main shutoff?
  • Who owns the building, and who authorizes the repair?
  • Is there someone who can give us access to the basement and to each unit?

What landlords and owners should plan for

A leak in one apartment often shows up in another, so finding the real source comes before the repair. A shared drain line that backs up affects every unit connected to it. Tenants usually need notice before anyone enters, and many leases say so. Keep records of each visit by unit so invoices are easy to match to each apartment.

See leak detection for locating a hidden leak and drain cleaning for shared lines. When pipes need replacing across a building, repiping explains what the work involves.

History and reference points

A horsecar line connected Highwood to downtown New Haven by 1870, and in the 1880 census it was the largest of Hamden's five villages. The Farmington Canal Heritage Trail runs through it, and the Keefe Community Center and John L. Carusone Field are local landmarks.

Highwood Square is a mixed-use development, and the former Newhall Street School now houses a business incubator. Dixwell Avenue and Newhall Street help us place an address quickly. When you call, a cross street such as Putnam Avenue, Fitch Street or Arch Street is enough for us to confirm the location.

Utilities and permits

Water in Hamden is supplied by the South Central Connecticut Regional Water Authority, which draws most of its supply from reservoirs. Much of the town's sewer system is operated by the Greater New Haven Water Pollution Control Authority. Plumbing work follows the 2022 State Building Code, based on the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC). The Hamden Permits and Licensing Center at 2750 Dixwell Avenue can tell you whether your job needs a permit.
