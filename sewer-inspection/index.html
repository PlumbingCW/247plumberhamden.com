A sewer camera inspection in Hamden, CT feeds a waterproof camera on a flexible cable into your main line and records what it sees. You get a look at pipe material, joints, cracks, roots, sags and any buildup, along with a plain-language explanation of what each finding means for you.

Most inspections are planned: before a home purchase, before a repair, or after a string of clogs. If a backup is happening now, our sewer line cleaning page covers emergency dispatch, and the camera comes afterward to confirm the pipe is sound.

What a camera can and cannot show

A camera shows the interior of the pipe. It identifies material such as clay, cast iron, PVC or Orangeburg, and it shows offset joints, cracks, roots, scale and standing water. A locator that rides with the camera lets us mark the surface above a problem so a repair does not require guessing.

It cannot show what is outside the pipe, such as soil conditions or a void, and it cannot inspect a line it cannot enter. A fully collapsed pipe stops the camera, and we mark the depth where it stopped.

Why people schedule one

Home buyers use inspections to learn what they are inheriting. A line that looks fine from the basement may have roots at the property line, and the difference can be a negotiation point or a reason to walk away. Sellers sometimes inspect first so they can price a home honestly.

Homeowners with repeat clogs use it to find out whether the cause is maintenance or damage. A clean line means a cleaning schedule, while a damaged one means repair is the real answer.

A note on older lines and Hamden's villages

Any older property, including those in villages such as Whitneyville and Centerville, is worth inspecting before a purchase if you do not know when the sewer line was last replaced. A house can be renovated several times without anyone touching the buried pipe.

That is a general reason to look, not a claim about any particular house. The camera tells you what is actually in the ground at your address.

Sewer or septic: what the camera covers

Many Hamden properties connect to the public sewer run by the Greater New Haven Water Pollution Control Authority, but properties in northern Hamden around Sleeping Giant State Park commonly rely on septic. We can camera the house line on a septic property to confirm it flows to the tank. The tank and field need a separate septic inspection.

For buyers, ask the seller which system the house uses. If the answer is unclear, the Town of Hamden can help you confirm sewer availability.

What you receive after the inspection

We save the recording and walk through it with you, pausing on each finding. You get a written summary of what we saw, the approximate location and depth of any problem, and a recommended next step. If the line is clean, we say so and stop there.

If you are in the middle of a sale, we can work to your timeline. Ask about scheduling when you call.

What the camera does and does not decide

A camera inspection reads the pipe condition. It does not approve or reject a line under the plumbing code. Connecticut's 2022 State Building Code is based on the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), and it is the standard a repair must meet afterward. If the video shows a defect, the next step is a repair plan that meets that code.
