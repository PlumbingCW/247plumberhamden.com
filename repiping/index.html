Whole-house repiping in Hamden, CT replaces the water supply piping throughout a home. It makes sense when pipes leak repeatedly, when water runs brown or weak because of corrosion inside, or when the material itself is known to fail, such as galvanized steel at the end of its life or polybutylene from a particular era of construction.

Repiping is a planned project, and we treat it that way. We give you a clear scope, a plan for protecting your home, and an honest view of whether a smaller repair would solve the problem. If a pipe has just burst, our burst pipe repair team handles the emergency first.

Signs your pipes may be due

The pattern matters more than any single event. One pinhole leak in a copper pipe is a repair. Three in two years is a signal. Rust-colored water at first draw, falling pressure that gets worse over time, and visible green corrosion at joints all point toward a system that is wearing out.

Galvanized steel corrodes from the inside, narrowing the pipe until pressure drops. Polybutylene, installed in many homes from roughly the late 1970s through the mid-1990s, has a record of failing at fittings. If you are not sure what you have, we can identify it on a short visit.

What the project looks like in a lived-in house

We plan around access. Basements, crawlspaces and attics let us run new pipe with minimal wall opening. Where walls must be opened, we choose small, strategic openings and plan the drywall patch. Most homeowners can stay in the house, with a few planned periods without water.

We sequence the work so the house is not without water for the whole project. New lines are connected, tested under pressure, and then the old lines are abandoned or removed.

Older neighborhoods and what we expect to find

Houses in neighborhoods such as Highwood and Spring Glen were built across many decades, and plumbing in any older house may have been modified more than once. Where several materials share a house, a repiping job is a chance to bring them all to one system.

That is a pattern we check for, not a claim about any specific house. A visit lets us see what you actually have.

Code, permits and inspection

Plumbing work in Connecticut falls under the 2022 Connecticut State Building Code, which is based on the 2021 International Plumbing Code. Permit rules for repiping vary, so ask the Hamden building department and check the town's permit portal for how a permit is requested.

We plan for inspection access, and we pressure test the new system before closing walls, so problems are found while they are easy to fix.

Repair now or repipe later

A single failed section in an otherwise sound system is a repair, and repiping is not always the answer. Our leak detection work can find and isolate the problem pipe, and you can decide from there.

If you are planning a kitchen or bathroom remodel, repiping at the same time is easier and cheaper than opening the same walls twice. Tell us about your plans when you call.

Code and inspection for new supply lines

Replacement supply piping is regulated. The 2022 Connecticut State Building Code is based on the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), which covers approved materials, sizing and testing.
