Cheshire is the northern neighbor of Hamden, and the first question we ask on a Cheshire call is whether your home is on the town sewer or on septic. The answer changes the repair. We answer 24/7 for ZIP 06410 and try to book the earliest same-day visit, including nights and weekends.

This page starts with that question and then covers what else sets Cheshire apart.

Sewer or septic: the question that decides the repair

The Cheshire Water Pollution Control Division provides sanitary sewer service to about 5,000 residential, commercial and industrial properties. The town had 10,401 housing units at the 2020 Census, so a good number of Cheshire homes are probably not on the municipal sewer and rely on septic systems.

A backup on a town sewer is a drain or lateral problem that we clear with a machine or a camera, and our sewer line cleaning page covers it. A backup on a septic system can mean a full tank or a failed drain field, which needs a septic specialist. We tell you honestly which one we see.

Water: the Regional Water Authority and its wells

The South Central Connecticut Regional Water Authority reports that it pumps about 12 percent of its supply from wells in Cheshire, Hamden, Derby and Seymour, and its report lists Cheshire among the towns served. Cheshire homes that connect to that system share the same utility questions as Hamden.

If your pressure drops or the meter runs with every tap closed, see our leak detection page. For a buried line that is leaking, water line repair covers the options.

A town founded in 1780 and its older homes

Cheshire was settled in 1694 as part of Wallingford and incorporated in 1780, and its center includes the Cheshire Historic District and Cheshire Academy, founded in 1794. Homes near the center can be old and have updated plumbing in sections.

If you are buying or inspecting an older house, a sewer camera inspection before closing shows the pipe condition, and it is useful negotiating information.

The Farmington Canal Trail and Roaring Brook Falls

The Farmington Canal Heritage Trail runs through Cheshire, and Roaring Brook Falls is the state's tallest single-drop waterfall. Both sit in a landscape of streams and slopes, where surface water finds its way toward foundations after heavy rain.

That makes a sump pump worth checking each spring. Our sump pump team tests and replaces pumps, and permit rules vary, so ask the town before any excavation. Plumbing work follows the 2022 Connecticut State Building Code, based on the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC).
