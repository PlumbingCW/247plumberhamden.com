Wallingford is the larger town just north of Hamden, with its own water division, its own municipal electric utility and a mix of very old and fairly new housing. When a pipe bursts or a water heater fails in ZIP 06492, we answer 24/7 and try for a same-day visit, day or night.

This page explains what the town's own setup means for plumbing calls and what to do before a plumber arrives.

Town water, wells and why it matters at your tap

The Wallingford Water Division reports that about 98 percent of its supply comes from four surface reservoirs in Wallingford, Durham and Guilford, with the rest from three groundwater sources in the Quinnipiac River Valley. Its 13,398 customers represent roughly 88 percent of the town's population, so a meaningful share of households are not on the municipal system.

That split affects repairs. If you are on town water, pressure and shutoff questions go to the Water Division. If you are on a private well, the pump, tank and pipes are all yours, and our water line repair team can help trace a problem. The annual report does not give a hardness figure, so ask the division or test your tap.

From 1667 to today: old centers and newer subdivisions

Wallingford was settled in 1667 and has a Wallingford Center Historic District, so some homes near the center are very old and have been modified many times. Villages such as Yalesville, East Wallingford and Tracy grew at different times, and the pipe materials inside the walls vary house to house.

If your house is old and you have seen rusty water or repeated pinhole leaks, our repiping page lays out your options. If the problem is a single leak, leak detection finds it before any wall is opened.

Wallingford's municipal utilities

Wallingford runs its own water, sewer and electric services. The Wallingford Electric Division, established in 1899, is one of the few municipal electric utilities in Connecticut. For a plumbing emergency this has one practical meaning: call the town utility for water and power questions, and call us for the plumbing inside your property line.

If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 before anyone else. Plumbing inside the property follows the 2022 Connecticut State Building Code, which is based on the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC).
