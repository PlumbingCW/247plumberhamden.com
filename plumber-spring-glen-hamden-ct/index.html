Spring Glen is a neighborhood in the southeastern part of Hamden. It has no official boundaries. The Spring Glen Civic Association describes it as running from Skiff Street south to Waite Street, between Lake Whitney and the Mill River on the west and Hartford Turnpike on the east. A small part of it lies in North Haven. Most of it uses ZIP code 06517.

It is primarily residential, mostly single-family homes with a few two-family houses. Whitney Avenue is its principal street and the only place with commercial development.

A neighborhood built in stages

Spring Glen's history is a good guide to how old your house might be. The table lists the milestones that shaped where and when homes appeared.

YearWhat happened
1745A road on the ridge north of East Rock, now Ridge Road, was laid out to reach area farms.
1800The Cheshire Turnpike, now Whitney Avenue, was built.
1858James J. Webb bought a farm he named Spring Glen, which gave the neighborhood its name.
1902The Whitney Avenue trolley reached Centerville, which led to the first house subdivision.
1924Hamden built the first Spring Glen School, which prompted more subdivisions.
1967The neighborhood was complete, developed as a trolley suburb of New Haven.

Source: the Spring Glen neighborhood entry on Wikipedia, which cites the Civic Association.

What this means for plumbing decisions

A house built in the 1920s and one built in the 1960s can stand a few streets apart, and renovations since then may have replaced some of the plumbing already. That is why we ask when the house was built, what has been replaced and what you can see in the basement before recommending anything.

If your water heater is more than a decade old, a planned swap is easier than a failure on a cold morning. See water heater replacement for what that involves.

If your water heater starts leaking

A leaking tank is one of the most common reasons homeowners call a plumber, and these steps limit the damage while you wait.

  1. Turn off the cold water supply valve above the heater.
  2. Turn off the power at the breaker, or set a gas heater to pilot or off.
  3. Keep people and pets away from hot water and wet electrical equipment.
  4. Put a bucket under the leak and call a plumber.

Sewer, septic and where your pipes go

Water in Hamden is supplied by the South Central Connecticut Regional Water Authority, which draws most of its supply from reservoirs. Much of the town's sewer system is operated by the Greater New Haven Water Pollution Control Authority. The small North Haven portion of Spring Glen is served by that town for municipal services, so a sewer question there goes to North Haven.

If you are not sure whether your street is on the municipal sewer, ask the town or check your property records. Plumbing questions inside the house are ours to answer. See sewer camera inspection if you want to see the line itself. Plumbing work in Connecticut follows the 2022 State Building Code, which is based on the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC).
