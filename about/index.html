About Hamden 24/7 Plumbing

Hamden 24/7 Plumbing is a call-only plumbing dispatch service in Hamden, Connecticut. You call one number, a person answers at any hour and a licensed plumber is sent to your address. We built the service around the calls homeowners actually make: a backed-up drain, a failed water heater, a burst pipe, a leak that will not stop.

We keep the process simple. You describe the problem, we help you stop the water if needed, and we explain the work and the price before anyone starts. We do not use forms, and we do not promise arrival times we cannot keep.

How a call works

First, we ask what you see and hear, where the main shutoff is and whether anyone is at risk. If there is a safety concern, such as a gas smell or water near electrical equipment, we tell you to leave the area and call the utility or 911 before we continue.

Next, we plan the earliest same-day visit the schedule allows. The plumber diagnoses the problem, explains the repair options and gives a price before work begins. Finally, the repair is tested before the plumber leaves.

What to have ready when you call

A few details make the call faster and the visit more useful. You do not need all of them, and we will help with whatever you do not know.

Helpful details

  • Your street address and the nearest cross street.
  • What you see, hear or smell, and when it started.
  • Whether water is still flowing and whether you could reach the main shutoff.
  • The age and type of the fixture or appliance, such as a gas or electric water heater.
  • Whether the property is a house, a rental or a business, and who controls access.

What we do not do

We repair and install plumbing. We do not pump septic tanks, repair foundations or dry out and rebuild water-damaged rooms, and we say so when a job needs a specialist. We would rather refer you than do a job badly.

We also do not state a licence number or a named owner on this site. The service dispatches licensed plumbers, and you can verify any Connecticut plumber by searching the state's eLicense portal, run by the Department of Consumer Protection.

How pricing works

Every job is priced by scope. The plumber looks at what failed, how hard it is to reach, what parts are needed and whether the visit is an emergency or a scheduled one. You hear the price before the work starts, and you can say no at that point.

Our service pages list the factors for each repair, so you can see what moves a price before you call. Insurance usually covers sudden water damage but not wear, so we suggest photographing any damage and calling your insurer early.

How to read any plumbing quote

A good quote names the problem, the repair and the parts, and it says what is not included. Be wary of a price quoted before anyone has looked at the problem, and of a plumber who will not put the scope in writing. Ask what warranty covers the parts and the labor, and ask what happens if the first repair does not hold.

For larger jobs such as a water heater replacement, a sewer repair or repiping, it is reasonable to ask for the repair and the replacement price side by side. We are happy to explain either, and our service pages describe the choices in plain terms.

Where we work

Our base is on Whitney Avenue in Hamden. We cover Hamden and the five towns that border it: North Haven, Wallingford, New Haven, Woodbridge and Cheshire.

Inside Hamden we work in every village, from Whitneyville near Lake Whitney and Centerville along Dixwell Avenue to Mount Carmel, Spring Glen and Highwood. Our ZIP coverage in town includes 06514, 06517 and 06518.

Our most requested work is drain cleaning, water heater repair and leak detection. The full service list shows everything we handle.

Why we publish local guides

Plumbing advice is only useful when it fits the place. Our service and town pages cite public sources. These include the South Central Connecticut Regional Water Authority's water quality report, the Greater New Haven Water Pollution Control Authority and the 2022 Connecticut State Building Code, which is based on the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC). They say plainly when a fact is not published. Where we do not know, we tell you to ask the utility or the town.

If you spot something on this site that looks wrong, call or email us and we will check it.
