Water Heater Pressure Relief Valve Repair for Hamden, CT Homes

The temperature and pressure relief valve is the most important safety device on your water heater. Know how it works and what a leak means.

Every tank water heater has a temperature and pressure relief valve, usually called a T&P valve, on the side or top of the tank. Its job is to open if the tank gets too hot or the pressure climbs too high. When it works, you never think about it. When it drips, it is telling you something.

This guide explains why a relief valve leaks, what you can safely check and when to call our water heater repair team.

Relief valve discharging steady water or steam

A valve that is blowing hot water or steam is an emergency. We answer 24/7 and dispatch around the clock, and we try for the earliest same-day visit.

Take these steps while you wait.

  1. Stay clear of the discharge pipe, since the water can be scalding.
  2. Turn the water heater thermostat to its lowest setting or switch off the gas control or the breaker.
  3. Close the cold water supply valve to the tank if you can reach it safely.
  4. Do not cap or plug the valve or its discharge pipe, because that removes the safety function.

What the valve does

Heating water makes it expand. In a closed system the pressure rises, and the T&P valve opens at a set limit to release it. It also opens if the water temperature passes a high limit. A discharge pipe carries the water to a safe spot near the floor.

Because it protects against a tank failure, never plug it, never remove it and never replace it with a cap. If it is opening regularly, something in the system needs attention.

Why a relief valve drips

A safe test of the valve

Place a bucket under the discharge pipe and lift the test lever for a second. Water should flow and stop when you let go. If it keeps dripping, the seat is dirty or damaged. If nothing flows, the valve may be stuck, which is a safety risk.

Only do this test if the discharge pipe points down toward a safe place. The water can be hot, and a valve that sticks open is better left to a plumber.

Expansion tanks and why they matter

In many homes the water meter or a check valve closes the system, so expanding water has nowhere to go. An expansion tank gives it room. If you have one, check the air charge with a tire gauge once a year.

A relief valve that drips after each heating cycle is a classic sign of a waterlogged or missing expansion tank. The fix is installing or replacing the tank, not the valve.

Repair or replace the heater

A new valve is a small part. If the tank is more than ten years old, rusted at the base or leaking at the seams, replacing the heater is usually the better decision. Our water heater replacement page explains how we size and install a new unit.

We serve New Haven and Cheshire as well as Hamden.

Maintenance that keeps the valve working

Once a year, check the valve for corrosion or mineral crust at the outlet, and make sure the discharge pipe is intact, points downward and ends within a few inches of the floor or a drain. Do not thread or cap the outlet, and do not run the discharge pipe uphill.

Draining a few gallons from the tank each year flushes sediment that can affect the temperature sensor. If you are not sure how to drain the tank safely, a plumber can do it during a regular visit and check the expansion tank and the thermostat at the same time.

Connecticut code and water heater safety

Plumbing work in Connecticut falls under the 2022 State Building Code, which is based on the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC). That code covers relief valves and their discharge piping, which is one reason replacement should be done correctly.

Permit rules vary, so ask the Hamden Permits and Licensing Center whether a water heater swap needs one. To verify any plumber's license, use the state's eLicense portal run by the Department of Consumer Protection.
