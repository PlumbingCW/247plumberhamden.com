Whitneyville is a neighborhood in southeastern Hamden. Hamden neighborhoods have no official boundaries, but one common map runs Whitneyville from Waite Street and the north end of Lake Whitney south to the New Haven line. The Mill River and Hartford Turnpike mark the east side, and the Farmington Canal Heritage Trail marks the west. Most of it uses ZIP code 06517. Just north, Spring Glen follows the same lake and river.

This page is for homeowners and landlords here who want a plumber's view of the neighborhood. We list what is documented about the area and then explain how to approach common jobs. We do not claim that homes here share any particular problem, because that depends on your own house.

How Whitneyville grew and what it means for your house

Eli Whitney bought a mill site on the Mill River in 1798 for his gun factory and built housing for his workers, which is how the village began. Lake Whitney was created around 1859 and 1860 when the mill dam was raised. Around 1900 a trolley line from New Haven reached the area, and surrounding land was subdivided for homes. The street network was substantially complete by 1927.

For a homeowner the practical point is simple. Houses in one village were built in different decades, so two neighbors can have different pipes. The build year on your property record, a look at your basement and a camera inspection of the sewer line tell you more than the neighborhood name does.

Four things to find out about your own house

These four answers shape almost every plumbing decision for an older or renovated house.

  1. The year it was built, from the town assessor's property record.
  2. The age of your water heater, which is printed on its data label.
  3. Where the main water shutoff sits and whether the valve turns freely.
  4. When the sewer line from the house to the street was last inspected or replaced, if anyone knows.

Water, sewer and the lake next door

Water in Hamden is supplied by the South Central Connecticut Regional Water Authority, which draws most of its supply from reservoirs. Much of the town's sewer system is operated by the Greater New Haven Water Pollution Control Authority. The line from a house to the sewer main is generally the owner's responsibility, and the main under the street belongs to the authority. A sewer camera inspection shows which side of the property line a problem is on.

Lake Whitney and the Mill River run along the neighborhood's edge. If your lot is on lower ground near either, watch for damp walls and floors after heavy rain and ask about a working sump pump. Not every basement here is affected, and we do not assume yours is.

Permits and the plumbing code

Plumbing work in Connecticut follows the 2022 State Building Code, which is based on the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC). Whether a particular job needs a permit is a question for the Hamden Permits and Licensing Center at Town Hall, 2750 Dixwell Avenue, 203-287-7000. You can also look up any plumber's license through the state's eLicense portal.
