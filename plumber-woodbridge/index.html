Woodbridge borders Hamden to the west, and its hills, ledges and larger lots make plumbing calls different from the ones in a dense town. When a pipe freezes in January or a sump pump fails in a spring storm, we answer 24/7 for ZIP 06525 and try for the earliest same-day visit.

This page is organized by season, because Woodbridge's plumbing problems follow the calendar.

Winter: frozen pipes on exposed walls

Woodbridge's elevation is about 348 feet, higher than much of the coast, and the West Rock ridge runs along its eastern edge. Homes on open lots take the wind on exterior walls, which is where pipes in crawlspaces and garages freeze first.

If water stops at a faucet in a cold snap, open the tap, apply gentle heat toward the frozen section and call us before it thaws. Our burst pipe repair page explains what to do when a pipe has already split.

Spring: runoff, sump pumps and wet basements

Snowmelt and heavy rain put water against foundations built into hillsides. A sump pump that works in the dry season may not keep up when the ground is saturated, and a power outage during a storm stops an electric pump entirely.

A battery backup keeps the pit from overflowing when the lights go out. Our sump pump repair team installs them, and we can discuss basement waterproofing if the water keeps coming.

Local facts that shape our calls.

Water and sewer: confirm what you have

The Regional Water Authority's report lists Woodbridge among the towns it serves, and the Greater New Haven Water Pollution Control Authority lists Woodbridge among the communities it provides sewer service to. That does not mean every property in a hilly, low-density town is connected to either one.

Some Woodbridge homes may rely on a private well or a septic system, and the plumber's job changes completely depending on which you have. Tell us when you call, and if you are unsure, the town can confirm. Our sewer line cleaning page covers municipal lines.

Woodbridge Green and older houses

The Woodbridge Green Historic District was listed on the National Register in 2003, and the Darling House dates from 1774. Houses near the Green can be very old, which often means original supply lines and drains that were extended many times.

If you own an older home and plan a bathroom or kitchen project, bring us in early. Our plumbing installation team can plan rough-in before walls close, and permit rules vary, so ask the town. Replacement piping follows the 2022 Connecticut State Building Code, based on the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC).
