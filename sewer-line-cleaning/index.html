Sewer line cleaning in Hamden, CT clears the main drain that carries every fixture's waste out of your house. When that line is partly blocked, you notice it everywhere: tubs draining slowly, toilets that gurgle when the washer runs, and a floor drain that burps. When it is fully blocked, sewage can rise in the lowest drain in the house.

We treat a full blockage as an emergency and answer the phone around the clock. A main line backup can contaminate a basement quickly, and getting water moving again is the priority before anything else.

Sewage coming up: start here

If sewage is surfacing in a tub, shower or basement floor drain, stop adding water and call. We dispatch 24/7, including nights and weekends, and we book the earliest same-day visit available.

Here is what to do while you wait.

  1. Do not flush, run the dishwasher or do laundry until the line is cleared.
  2. Keep children and pets away from the affected area and wear boots if you must enter.
  3. Turn off power to a flooded basement only if you can reach the panel without standing in water. Otherwise leave it to the utility or an electrician.
  4. Photograph the damage for your records before cleaning up, in case you file a claim.

How a main line gets cleaned

Access comes first. Most homes have a cleanout, a capped pipe near the foundation or in the basement, that lets us feed a cable or jetting hose directly into the main. If there is no accessible cleanout, we can work from a toilet flange or a roof vent, which takes longer.

We cut through the blockage, then flush the line so loosened debris does not settle a few feet downstream. Afterward we run water through the system and listen for gurgling, which tells us whether the line is flowing or only partly open.

What usually blocks a main line

The usual causes are tree roots, grease that collected at a bend, wipes and other products that do not break down, and a sagging section where solids settle. Roots are the most common reason a line that was cleaned last year is blocked again.

Wipes labeled as flushable are a quiet cause of repeat blockages. They look harmless, but they do not break apart like toilet paper and they tangle on roots, rough joints and each other.

Sewer connections in and around Hamden

Public sewer in Hamden is operated by the Greater New Haven Water Pollution Control Authority, which also serves New Haven, East Haven and Woodbridge. Your private lateral, the pipe from the house to the connection, is typically the property owner's responsibility, so confirm the dividing line with the authority if a blockage appears to be out in the street.

Not every Hamden property is on a sewer. Parts of northern Hamden near Sleeping Giant State Park use private septic. If you are not sure which you have, tell us when you call so we begin with the right diagnosis.

Preventing the next backup

Prevention is mostly habit. Keep grease out of the drain, flush only toilet paper, and avoid planting trees or shrubs directly over the line. If the camera showed roots, schedule cleaning before they reach the same density again.

For rental or multi-unit buildings, regular cleaning on a schedule is cheaper than repeated emergency calls. Our commercial plumbing page covers maintenance options for shared properties.

Code and public sewer limits

A building sewer connects to the public main under rules the town and the sewer authority set, and the plumbing code covers the pipe on your side. Connecticut's 2022 State Building Code is based on the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC). Clearing a lateral does not change it, but replacing a section does, so ask the Hamden Permits and Licensing Center before any excavation.
