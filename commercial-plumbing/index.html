Commercial plumbing in Hamden, CT serves restaurants, small offices, retail shops, multi-unit rentals and light industrial spaces along Whitney Avenue and the Dixwell Avenue (Route 10) corridor. When a plumbing problem closes a kitchen or floods an office, the clock matters, and so does a plumber who understands that.

We answer 24/7 and dispatch around the clock for business emergencies. Tell us the building type and what happened, and we will plan around your operating hours wherever we can.

Business emergency: sewage, flooding or no water

Sewer backups, burst pipes and a loss of water can close a business and trigger health rules. We answer day and night, and we aim for the earliest same-day visit.

Take these steps while you wait.

  1. Close the main shutoff valve if water is flowing, and keep customers and staff out of wet or sewage-affected areas.
  2. Stop using sinks, toilets and floor drains if sewage is backing up.
  3. Photograph the damage and note times for your insurance claim.
  4. For a restaurant or a food business, check with the local health department about reopening rules.

Drains, grease and backups

Commercial drains carry more load than household lines, and grease is the biggest enemy in food service. Regular hydro jetting clears buildup that an auger only pokes through, and it can be scheduled outside business hours.

For a recurring backup, a camera inspection finds the cause. See sewer inspection for what a camera shows.

Water heaters for business

A commercial water heater is a daily-use appliance, and a failed unit can stop a kitchen or a salon. We repair and replace tank and tankless heaters, and we talk through capacity and recovery before recommending a size. Our water heater replacement page covers the process.

For a rental building, the question is often whether one central unit or individual units suit the layout.

Landlords, property managers and rentals

Hamden has rental properties, from two- and three-family houses to small apartment buildings, including in Highwood. Landlords often need reliable response, clear invoices per unit and coordination with tenants about access. Tell us the number of units, the contact for access and any shutoff locations.

We keep notes on repeat issues so you can see patterns, such as a stack that clogs often, and fix the cause instead of the symptom.

Codes, inspections and backflow

Commercial plumbing falls under the 2022 Connecticut State Building Code, which is based on the 2021 International Plumbing Code. Some systems, such as backflow preventers, need periodic testing. Ask your local officials which testing applies to your building.

Permit rules for commercial work vary, so confirm with the Hamden building department before starting larger projects.

What to do before help arrives at a business

These steps protect staff, customers and the building.

  2. Stop using sinks, toilets and floor drains if sewage is backing up.
  3. Photograph the damage and note times for your insurer.
