Sump pump repair in Hamden, CT is a call that spikes when heavy rain arrives and a pump fails to keep up. A sump pump sits in a pit in the basement floor, collects groundwater and pushes it out and away from the foundation. When it stops, the pit overflows and the basement floods.

If water is rising in the pit or on the floor, call right away. We answer 24/7 and dispatch day and night, including storm nights, because that is when sump pumps fail.

Pit full or basement flooding: do this now

Water in a basement is an emergency, and storm nights are when we are busiest. We aim for the earliest same-day visit and we will guide you by phone.

Safety first.

  1. Do not enter a flooded basement if water is above outlets or appliances, and do not touch electrical equipment while standing in water.
  2. Turn off power at the breaker only if you can reach the panel safely from dry ground. If not, call the electric utility.
  3. Check that the pump is plugged in and the outlet and breaker are on, and that the discharge pipe outside is not frozen or blocked.
  4. Move belongings off the floor, and photograph the water level and damage.

Why sump pumps fail

Most failures are predictable, and many are preventable.

Battery backup and why it matters

Storms that bring heavy rain often bring power outages too, which is exactly when an electric pump stops. A battery backup pump runs on a separate battery and takes over when the main pump or the power fails. A water-powered backup uses pressure from the municipal supply, which can work well but uses water and depends on supply pressure.

We help you choose between the options based on your pit, your discharge and how much you rely on the pump.

Replacing a pump or installing a new system

A pump has a limited life, and one that is ten years old or showing rust, noise or short cycling is worth replacing before it fails. A new installation includes digging the pit, setting the pump, plumbing the discharge to a safe location and checking the check valve.

Discharge location matters. Water should go well away from the house and not toward a neighbor. Check local rules before connecting a sump pump to a sanitary sewer, since many places forbid it. We can help you find where to discharge.

Hamden's water table and low spots

Parts of Hamden, including Whitneyville, sit near the Mill River and Lake Whitney, and homes on low ground or at the base of slopes can see groundwater rise after prolonged rain. Whether your basement is affected depends on your own lot and foundation, so look for the signs before the next storm.

A pump is a response to groundwater, not a cure. If you see recurring water, our basement waterproofing page explains drainage options that reduce the load on the pump.

Maintenance that prevents flooded basements

Test your pump twice a year by pouring a bucket of water into the pit and watching it start, discharge and stop. Clean the intake, check the check valve and make sure the outdoor discharge is clear of leaves and ice.

Before winter, make sure the discharge line drains and does not freeze, since a frozen line stops the pump from moving water out.

Where sump water may go

Discharge rules matter as much as the pump. Many places forbid sending sump water into a sanitary sewer, and the plumbing code covers the pipe and the check valve. Connecticut's 2022 State Building Code is based on the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC). Ask the Hamden Permits and Licensing Center before connecting a discharge line.
