Hydro jetting in Hamden, CT is the service we recommend when a line keeps clogging after cabling, or when a main drain backs up and the blockage is more than a single wad of debris. A jetting hose carries a nozzle that fires water at high pressure against the pipe wall, so it cleans the full circumference rather than boring a hole through the middle of the blockage.

Callers often tell us the last plumber snaked the line and it ran fine for a few weeks. That pattern points to buildup that was pushed aside, not removed. Jetting is built for exactly that, and it is available on our 24/7 line when a backup cannot wait.

Sewer backup: what to do first

A main line that is backing up into tubs and floor drains is an emergency, and jetting is often the fastest way to restore flow once we confirm the line is intact. We take these calls at night and on weekends, and we aim for a same-day visit whenever the schedule allows.

Until we arrive, protect the house with these steps.

  1. Stop using water. A single flush can send more sewage into the lowest fixture.
  2. Keep everyone out of the affected area and away from standing water, which may carry bacteria.
  3. If a floor drain is overflowing, move belongings out of the way and avoid touching electrical items in the room.
  4. Tell us where the main cleanout is if you can find it, because it saves time on arrival.

What jetting removes that a cable leaves behind

A cable cuts a channel. Jetting rinses the wall. In a kitchen line, that means cooking grease that has hardened in layers. In an older cast iron line, it can mean scale that has narrowed the pipe over years. In a clay or concrete line, it can mean fine roots that grow back as soon as the cut ends are left in place.

Pressure is matched to the pipe. We use lower settings and gentler nozzle heads for older or fragile lines, because high pressure on damaged pipe can make a problem worse. That is one reason we look at the line before we commit to jetting.

Why we put a camera on the line before and after

Jetting without looking is guesswork. A camera shows whether the pipe is intact, whether the blockage is roots or grease, and whether there is a belly that will keep catching waste no matter how clean the wall is. We also use it afterward to confirm the pipe is actually clear.

That recording is yours to keep. If the footage shows damage, you have the evidence to decide on a repair, and our sewer camera inspection page explains what you will see.

Where jetting makes sense around Hamden

Hamden's sewer laterals run from the house to a main that the Greater New Haven Water Pollution Control Authority operates. Mature trees on residential lots can send roots toward the joints of older lines, and that is as true in Mount Carmel or Spring Glen as anywhere else. If you see tree roots on the camera, jetting after a root cut gives the line its best chance of staying clear.

Commercial kitchens along busy corridors such as Dixwell Avenue and Whitney Avenue deal with grease loads that residential lines never see. Our commercial plumbing team uses jetting as scheduled maintenance for restaurants and shared buildings.

Limits we will tell you about

Jetting is not a repair. If a pipe is cracked, collapsed or separated at a joint, cleaning it does not fix it. We will say so, and we will show you the footage. Fragile or very old pipe may also not tolerate pressure, in which case cabling with a careful head is the better choice.

On septic systems, the first question is whether the problem is in the house line or in the tank and field. Jetting the house line will not fix a full tank.

Standards that apply to jetting

Jetting cleans existing pipe and does not change the system, but the work still sits inside the plumbing code. Connecticut's 2022 State Building Code is based on the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), which expects accessible cleanouts on drain lines. A line with no cleanout may need one added before jetting, and permit rules vary, so ask the town if you plan to add it.
