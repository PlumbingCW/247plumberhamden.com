Toilet repair in Hamden, CT usually starts with one of four problems: the bowl will not flush or keeps clogging, the tank keeps running, water leaks around the base, or the toilet rocks and will not stay sealed to the floor. Each has a different cause and a different fix, and guessing wastes a weekend.

An overflowing toilet, or one that backs up with the water still rising, is an emergency. We answer 24/7 and dispatch day and night, so call at any hour.

Toilet overflowing: stop the water first

If water is rising or spilling onto the floor, act before you call. Our line is open around the clock for overflows, and we aim for the earliest same-day visit.

Do these steps in order.

  1. Lift the tank lid and push the flapper down, or close the supply valve on the wall behind the toilet by turning it clockwise.
  2. Do not flush again. A second flush adds water to a bowl that is already full.
  3. Put towels around the base and move rugs away. Keep the bathroom clear of electrical cords.
  4. If several drains are slow or other fixtures gurgle, the clog may be in the main line, so say that when you call. See drain cleaning.

A toilet that will not stop running

A running toilet can waste a surprising amount of water and quietly raise a bill. The usual culprits are a flapper that no longer seals, a fill valve that does not shut off, or a chain that is too tight or too loose. Parts are inexpensive, and a repair often takes a single visit.

To check yourself, add a few drops of food coloring to the tank and wait fifteen minutes without flushing. Color in the bowl means the flapper leaks. If the water level sits at the top of the overflow tube, the fill valve needs adjustment or replacement.

Clogs, augers and when it is deeper than the toilet

Most clogs sit in the toilet's own trap, and a closet auger clears them without scratching the porcelain. When a toilet clogs again within days, or when a tub, floor drain or sink also acts up, the problem is farther down the line. We bring a drain machine and, when needed, a camera.

Please do not flush wipes labeled flushable, paper towels or feminine products. They do not break down like toilet paper and are a leading cause of repeat blockages in household lines and in the sewer system.

Leaks at the base and the wax ring

A toilet seals to the floor flange with a wax or foam ring. If that seal fails, water seeps out at the base during or after a flush, and over time it can rot the subfloor. Replacing the ring means removing the toilet, inspecting the flange, and resetting the bowl on a new seal.

If the floor around the toilet feels soft or spongy, tell us. Subfloor damage is a bigger repair, and the sooner it is spotted the smaller it stays.

Repair or replace

A toilet that is cracked, that has a damaged flange or that keeps failing in spite of repairs is a candidate for replacement. Newer models use less water per flush, and many fit the same opening. We can install one you have bought, or help you pick one that is easy to service.

Older Hamden bathrooms

In an older home, a slow toilet can come from the drain rather than the bowl. A camera inspection tells us whether the drain, not the toilet, is the cause.

Code points for toilets

Toilet installation falls under the plumbing code, which sets requirements for clearances, flush volume and sealing to the drain. Connecticut's 2022 State Building Code is based on the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC). Routine repairs such as a flapper or a fill valve are maintenance, while resetting a toilet on a new flange is plumbing work, so ask the town if you are unsure.
