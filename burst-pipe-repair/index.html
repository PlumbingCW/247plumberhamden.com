Burst pipe repair in Hamden, CT is a call we expect most often when a hard freeze hits and a pipe splits in an exterior wall, a crawlspace or an unheated corner of the basement. It can also be a failed fitting, a corroded section or a supply line that gave way under pressure. In every case the first job is the same: stop the water.

We answer 24/7 and run emergency burst pipe repair at any hour. If water is flowing now, close the main valve first, then call us. A burst pipe can release gallons every minute, so every minute spent finding the shutoff matters.

A pipe has burst: do this before we arrive

A burst pipe is an emergency at any hour. We dispatch day and night, we aim for a same-day visit, and we talk you through the shutoff over the phone if you cannot find it.

Work through these steps in order.

  1. Close the main water shutoff valve. It is usually where the line enters the house, often in the basement near the meter.
  2. Open the lowest cold faucet and flush the toilet once to drain remaining water from the pipes.
  3. Turn off the electricity to wet areas at the breaker if you can reach the panel without standing in water, and stay away from outlets and appliances that are wet.
  4. Move furniture, rugs and valuables out of the water, and photograph the damage for your insurance claim.

What causes pipes to burst

Freezing is the textbook cause, because water expands as it turns to ice and the pressure can split a pipe. The break often occurs in the section just downstream of the ice blockage, and it may not leak until the ice melts. That is why a house can seem fine during a cold snap and then flood on the first mild day.

Other causes include corrosion in older galvanized or copper pipe, failed fittings, water hammer, excessive pressure and damage from renovation. We look for the cause so the same pipe does not fail again next season.

Repair approaches by pipe type

The right repair depends on what the pipe is made of and how long the damaged section is.

Thawing a frozen pipe safely

If a pipe is frozen but has not burst, you may be able to thaw it. Open the faucet so water can flow as it melts, then apply gentle heat with a hair dryer or warm towels, working from the faucet back toward the frozen section. Never use an open flame, a torch or a space heater near wet materials.

If you cannot find the frozen section, or if you see a bulge, a crack or a wet spot, stop and call. A pipe that is already damaged can start leaking the moment it thaws.

Preventing the next freeze

Insulate pipes in unheated spaces, seal air leaks near exterior walls and crawlspaces, and keep the heat set high enough to protect them if you travel. On very cold nights, opening cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls lets warm air reach the pipes, and a slow drip can keep water moving.

Know where your main shutoff is and make sure it turns. A valve that has not been used in years can seize, and we can replace a stuck valve during the repair visit.

After the water stops

Once we repair the pipe, the cleanup begins. Drying and restoration of wet drywall, flooring and insulation is a separate job from plumbing. If a large area is wet, call a water damage restoration company promptly, because mold can start within a couple of days of persistent moisture.

We can also run a pressure test on the repaired section and, if needed, trace other leaks with our leak detection methods so you know the system is holding.

Local notes for Hamden homes

Exterior walls that take the full force of winter wind, as they can on exposed lots in places such as Mount Carmel near Sleeping Giant State Park, are where pipes freeze first. In any neighborhood, additions and converted porches are places where pipes can run through cold cavities.

That is a pattern to check, not a rule. If a bathroom or kitchen sits over a garage, a porch or a crawlspace, it is worth inspecting before winter.

Repairs and the plumbing code

A replacement section must meet the same plumbing code as the original. Connecticut's 2022 State Building Code is based on the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), which covers approved pipe materials and fittings, and we use fittings rated for the pipe type. Permit rules vary, so ask the Hamden Permits and Licensing Center if the repair is large.
